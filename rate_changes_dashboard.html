How is Electricity Affordability Evolving in the US?

A coarse-to-fine view: national trends → what utilities spend on → state averages → utility-level variation.
All figures in real 2024$ (CPI-U adjusted). Sources: EIA Form 861 & FERC Form 1, 2001–2024.

1. National Overview — Who Pays What
Since 2001, residential rates barely changed in real terms (+2%), while commercial (−21%) and industrial (−21%) rates fell sharply. Residential customers now pay 2.2× the industrial rate (up from 1.7×). Total sales grew 34%, driven by commercial (+53%) growth.
2. What Are Utilities Spending On? (FERC Form 1)
Investor-owned utilities spent $282B in 2023 (real 2024$). The biggest shifts since 2001:
• Purchased power fell from 57% to 27% of costs, then rebounded to 33% — reflecting deregulation cycles and wholesale price swings.
• Fuel costs spiked in 2008 (coal/gas peaks), fell with the shale gas revolution, then rose again in 2022.
• Depreciation nearly doubled ($26B → $47B) as utilities poured capital into grid modernization and renewables.
• Transmission tripled ($7B → $19B) — the fastest-growing category — driven by interconnection buildout and grid hardening.
3. State-Level View
Rates vary 4× across states (11¢ in ND to 43¢ in HI). Some saw 30–50% real increases since 2019 (LA, IN, KY); others declined (DC, TX, MI). Customer mix varies hugely: FL is 54% residential; VA is 58% commercial.
